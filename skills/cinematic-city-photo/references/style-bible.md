# Cinematic City Photo Style Bible

## Intent

Make an observed, editorial urban photograph: warm, cinematic, and specific to its place—not a preset-heavy vintage imitation. Default frame is vertical **4:5**; leave usable negative space for restrained cover typography when the story allows.

## Palette

Grade by relationship, not a fixed LUT. Let sunlit planes and practical lights carry amber and warm **gold**; allow restrained orange-red accents and muted yellow. Counterweight them with teal-green shadow detail and deep neutral blacks. Keep skin, sky, foliage, concrete, water, and signage credible for the scene: daylight can be clean and airy, overcast can be cooler and softer, coasts can retain blue-green air, and autumn can be warm without turning every leaf orange.

## Light and Tone

Prefer believable golden-hour sun, soft side light, or backlight that follows the original scene's direction. Preserve readable midtones and shadow detail; use controlled contrast, gentle highlight roll-off, and a modest vignette only when it supports attention. Avoid crushed blacks, clipped highlights, flat muddy shadows, and heavy **HDR** local contrast.

## Film Texture

Use subtle fine **grain**, gentle halation only around credible bright sources, and a soft optical falloff. Texture must read at normal viewing size, never obscure faces, signs, or location detail. Do not simulate coarse dirt, hard glow, fake bokeh, or an aggressive vintage wash.

## Composition

Favor strong geometry, leading lines, frames within frames, reflections, layered foreground, and small human figures for scale. Strengthen the frame with a crop or perspective correction only when it keeps the scene truthful. Protect faces, the key action, transit objects, architecture, storefronts, and distinctive signs; use clear negative space rather than covering them with title treatment.

## Realism Gate

The result should look like a skilled travel-documentary photograph made in the same place and moment. Preserve plausible weather, scale, shadows, reflections, textures, and depth. A cinematic grade enhances what is there; it does not invent an impossible sunset, replace a city, or make people look staged.

## Anti-patterns

- Orange skin, radioactive foliage, cyan skies, or over-saturated signage.
- Heavy HDR, crushed shadows, excessive bloom, or visible fake bokeh.
- Strong artificial grain, sepia wash, or uniform teal-and-orange treatment.
- Generic postcard staging that erases the city's cultural texture.
- Text over faces, focal objects, or meaningful location details.
