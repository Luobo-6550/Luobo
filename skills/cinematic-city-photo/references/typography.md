# Editorial Typography and Layout

Use typography as a quiet cover treatment, not as an overlay that competes with the photograph. Add it automatically when the image has a safe layout area; preserve a clean image when it does not.

## Copy Rules

- Main title: **one to four English words**, in title case. Derive it from a verified location, season, action, or visible subject: `Harbor Line`, `Rainy Kowloon`, `Sunday Market`.
- Subtitle: a smaller **UPPERCASE** English descriptor, usually two or three words: `CITY NOTES`, `AUTUMN STORIES`, `SEOUL SERIES`.
- If the location is not known from the source or request, do not invent a city. Use a mood or scene title such as `After Rain`, `Last Light`, `Quiet Crossing`, or `Coastal Air`.
- Keep wording editorial, brief, and specific. Do not reproduce long signs, add dates, or use decorative slogans.

## Type Treatment

- Set the main title in an editorial serif with moderate weight and relaxed tracking; it should feel like a travel-photography cover, not a poster headline.
- Set the subtitle in a restrained, small sans-serif with wider tracking. Keep it visually subordinate to the title.
- Use one color only for the title/subtitle pair: **yellow, orange, dark red, or white**. Select the color by local contrast, not by a fixed brand color.
- Preserve legibility with clean letterforms and moderate size; avoid thick outlines, drop shadows, glow, warped type, and dense text blocks.

## Deterministic Placement and Collision Check

1. Inspect the final 4:5 frame and identify candidate negative-space zones: upper left, upper right, lower left, lower right, then a plain edge band. A candidate must be visually quiet and separated from the story.
2. Reject a zone if the title or subtitle would overlap, touch, or materially obscure a face, person, focal object, vehicle, key action, storefront, sign, logo, landmark, horizon feature, or important location detail. Do not create space by removing meaningful people or place details.
3. Choose the first remaining zone in this priority order: upper left, upper right, lower left, lower right, clear edge band. Align both lines to the same edge and keep a modest inset from the frame.
4. Test local contrast behind the complete text block. Choose white on a darker/cooler area; yellow or orange on deep neutral, teal-green, or shadowed areas; dark red on pale warm sky, concrete, or other light backgrounds. If the selected color is weak, try the other permitted colors before moving the block.
5. If no candidate remains, reduce title size once and retest a clear edge placement. If it still collides or loses legibility, keep the clean image for this pass and use the terminal fallback below.

## Two-Pass Fallback

When generated lettering is malformed, misspelled, illegible, or crowded—or when no safe area exists—first generate or edit a **clean, title-free image**. For malformed text, use a separate typography pass that adds only the approved title and subtitle at the selected safe placement.

For a no-safe-text-area result after the smaller edge placement fails, use a separate typography/layout pass to create a restrained low-contrast tonal field or subtle gradient in the least-salient edge region, then place the title there. Do not remove, cover, or materially alter people, focal objects, signs, landmarks, or story details to make that field. Recheck spelling, contrast, and collisions after the pass. If even this treatment cannot make the title legible without affecting protected detail, deliver the clean image title-free and state why. For revisions, preserve an approved title and layout unless the requested change specifically concerns typography.
